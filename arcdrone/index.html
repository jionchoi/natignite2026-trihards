01 · The gap

Four million square kilometres of silence.

Canada’s Arctic remains the largest under-surveilled territory in the developed world. Satellite revisit times are slow, manned patrols are expensive and sparse, and existing radar coverage misses anything low, small, or quiet.

01
Persistent coverage doesn’t exist

Satellites pass overhead in minutes. Manned aircraft fly for hours, not days. Nothing watches continuously.

02
Low, slow, small targets slip through

Long-range radar misses small vessels, low-altitude aircraft, and ground intrusions. The threats that matter are exactly the ones unseen.

03
Legacy platforms cost too much to scale

A single MQ-style asset costs more than a fleet of distributed nodes. You can’t saturate four million km² with platforms that expensive.

02 · Architecture

04 · Applications

Built for defence. Useful far beyond it.

Defence · Sovereignty

Unauthorized Arctic entry detection

Continuous low-altitude coverage flags vehicles, vessels, and personnel crossing into sovereign zones — beneath radar, beyond satellites.

Defence · Air domain

Low-altitude aircraft intrusion

Detects the slow, small, and quiet — the exact threat profile legacy long-range radar systems were never tuned for.

Defence · Resources

Illegal resource activity

Persistent monitoring of remote mining sites and resource corridors — flags extraction, heavy equipment, and supply movement.

Critical Infrastructure

Pipeline & corridor monitoring

Cost-effective uptime watch on remote pipelines, transmission corridors, and rail — anomaly alerts before failures cascade.

Maritime · Climate

Ice & maritime route monitoring

Real-time ice movement, ship transit, and shipping lane awareness — including the rapidly opening Northwest Passage.

Public Safety

Search & rescue, extreme conditions

Thermal-equipped swarms canvas terrain humans can’t — minutes count, and a saturated swarm searches in parallel.
